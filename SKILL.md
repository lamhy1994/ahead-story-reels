# Ahead story video

Keith's house format for every video (approved 19 Nov 2026 on episode 1, "The King With Too Much Gold"; storytelling tightened on 22 Nov 2026; format v3 on 28 Nov 2026: hook lab, lesson card, cheat-sheet CTA, §3–4). Use it for any video he asks for, and for the scheduled daily runs. It replaces `kinetic-explainer-video`. Where `ai-reel-formula` disagrees about length, look, CTA or sound, this skill wins; its 底线 still hold (never say or show Bybit, no income promises, no logos or real app screens, facts need a source).

Code, fonts and the worked example live in the GitHub repo **lamhy1994/ahead-story-reels** (attach it with add_repo, push access; this file is its SKILL.md and is the newest version). Report to Keith in short Chinese; the video, captions and post are English only.

## 1. The format
- One true money story with a date, the people involved, what happened and how it ended (for news: where it stands today), told so that it teaches one lesson an ordinary person can use (§3). Newest hot news first, older history only when no news qualifies (§2).
- 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps. Length 45–60 s by default; 30–120 s allowed, over 75 s only when the story really needs it (`LONG = "<reason>"` in script.py) (Keith, 22 Nov 2026). Pace about 160 words a minute over the whole video. Tone: a story told by a warm narrator, brisk, never sleepy; not a news report or a lecture.
- The first 10 seconds come from the hook lab (Keith, 28 Nov 2026: test 100 kinds of openings until people watch to 10 seconds, and remember the formula): `python3 hooklab.py pick` chooses each episode's opening (§3.1). The opening Keith set on 22 Nov 2026 (the hook card over a wide shot of the main place for 1.5 s, then the big number for 1.5 s) is one of its combinations.
- Picture: a low-poly, flat-shaded, warm-coloured 3D world built in code (people as simple figures, camels, buildings, boats, coins). The camera tours the world like a guide walking the viewer through it. Something new moves or appears every 2–3 s. Keith, 22 Nov 2026: the picture style stays as it is.
- One take: no hard cuts. Change place with a sky wipe (camera tilts up into the sky, the sky changes, camera comes down somewhere new) or a crane move over the roofs.
- Where this is going (Keith, 28 Nov 2026): 100 million views and 100,000 followers. On 28 Nov 2026: 25 Reels, about 4,500 views, 9 followers, each Reel shown to about 130 people. Instagram shows a Reel to more people only when that first small audience stays and sends it on, so the work goes into the first 10 seconds, the lesson and the numbers (`trends/hooklab.md`), never into more Reels a day.

## 2. Picking the story: hottest recent news first (Keith, 20 Nov 2026)
1. Topics: crypto, stocks and companies, macro (rates, inflation, gold, currencies, trade as market news), scams and trader stories. English-language news from anywhere in the world.
2. Search the windows in this order and take the hottest qualifying story from the first window that has one: last 14 days → 1 month → 3 months → 1 year → 5 years → 20 years → older history (`ideas.md`).
3. "Hot" means all of: (a) covered by at least 3 major outlets (Reuters, Bloomberg, AP, FT, WSJ, CNBC, BBC; for crypto also CoinDesk, The Block); (b) clearly discussed: high-view YouTube videos, Reddit threads or X posts found by search; (c) vidIQ budget (Keith, 21 Nov 2026: up to 20 credits a day): one `vidiq_instagram_tiktok_outlier_search` (5 credits) per run, at most 20 credits a day across all runs. Log every paid call in `trends/<today, Malaysia time>.md` (time, query, credits, top results) and read that file first: reuse its results when they already cover your candidates, and never let the day's total go over 20. Call `vidiq_balance` first and skip vidIQ when the balance is under 300. No other paid vidIQ tool in story runs (no images, thumbnails, watch, comments, keyword research or profiles); `vidiq_video_upload`, `vidiq_instagram_publish_reel` and `vidiq_instagram_owner_insights` are free. Rank the candidates and note the evidence in `sources.md`.
4. Skip: stories mainly about politicians, elections or government policy fights (Instagram does not recommend political content to non-followers by default, and posting it can make the account ineligible for recommendations); anything involving Bybit; stories with only rumours or a single outlet behind them; anything where real victims would end up as a punchline.
5. A story that is still hot can get a new episode every run, but each episode needs a new angle and new facts (what happened · one person's story · the mechanism · how it compares with a past event · the latest twist). Record `story`, `angle` and `window` in episodes.json and never repeat a story + angle pair.
6. Facts. News: every number and claim from at least two independent reliable sources, primary where possible (company filings and statements, exchange or on-chain data, court records, official statistics; the CoinGecko connector for crypto prices). Prices and totals carry "as of <date>" on screen. Allegations stay allegations ("accused", "charged", "says"). History: primary or scholarly sources; where historians disagree the narrator attributes it ("one writer in Cairo said", "some old accounts say") and the footnote says the point is debated. Never present a legend, rumour or disputed number as fact. Note title, URL and date of every source in `sources.md` (WebSearch, then WebFetch the original page, not snippets).
7. Unfinished news: tell it up to today and end on "the story isn't over yet" with the date. Never predict a price or an outcome.
8. The lesson test (Keith, 24 Nov 2026: the stories did not teach ordinary people anything). Take a story only if (a) it has one person or company at its centre and something that should not have happened, and (b) it carries one lesson a person with a little money can use for their own next decision. The four Reels watched longest by 28 Nov 2026 (episodes 13, 6, 3 and 1) are all one character and one strange event; episodes that were mostly a mechanism lost viewers. So the mechanism goes inside the person's story, never the other way round. If the hottest story has no lesson, take the next one.

## 3. Script (script.py): a story that teaches, in seven parts (Keith, 28 Nov 2026)
Why: on 24 Nov 2026 the first 13 Reels had 2,930 plays, 1 comment and 9 followers. 32–51% of viewers left in the first 3 s and the average viewer was gone after 12–31 s, so about one viewer in ten was still there for the CTA; the lesson was one line near the end. The 22 Nov changes (hook card, big number, faster, shorter) did not help: by 28 Nov the 14 Reels made that way lost 42% in the first 3 s against 35% for the first eight. Keith sent Kallaway's script structure on 24 Nov 2026 (hook, lock-in, body blocks with one surprise each, rehooks, a CTA that gives something); episode 13, which happened to have two surprises and a "here's the twist" line, was watched 48% of the way, the others 13–32%. Live numbers: `trends/insights.md`, `trends/hooklab.md` (§9).

The seven parts (`PARTS` in script.py lists the sentences of each; `./tts.sh` prints when each starts). For 45–60 s: 120–165 words.
1. **hook** (0–3 s, one sentence, 14 words at most). From the hook lab (§3.1).
2. **lock** (the lock-in, 3–10 s, two sentences). Why the rest is worth watching: what this has to do with the viewer's own money, or what they will understand by the end. It ends on the question the video answers. No backstory yet. Over by 10 s; `tts.sh` stops at 12.5 s. Its style comes from the hook lab.
3. **body1** (3–5 sentences). What happened, to one person or company, in order, and it ends on surprise 1: something the viewer would not have guessed. Backstory only where it answers the open question, two sentences at most.
4. **rehook** (one sentence, 12 words at most, at 35–55% of the video). It opens a new question just as the first is answered: "But here's the part nobody expects." "That's not the strange part." The picture changes place or scale on it and a sound lands (§5–6).
5. **body2** (3–5 sentences). Surprise 2: how it works, made concrete with one tiny example in round numbers (episode 1: one coin buys 5 loaves, then 4). News then says where it stands today, dated ("the story isn't over yet").
6. **lesson** (1–2 sentences). "The lesson: …" is a rule about how money works that follows from the story, 12 words at most; then the check the viewer can use before their own next decision: a question to ask or a number to look up ("So before you buy anything rare, ask how much more is coming."). Never buy, sell or hold, never a promise, never a prediction. `LESSON` (2 lines, one `*amber*` word) and `LESSON_USE` put it on the lesson card (§6), built to be screenshotted and sent on.
7. **cta** (1–2 sentences, 4–7 s). §4.

Writing rules (from Kallaway's principles and our own numbers):
- A chain, never a list: every beat answers the open question and opens the next (but / so / then). Cut every sentence that does not move it.
- Clear: everyday words (reading grade 5 or below), active voice, one idea per sentence, at most one number per sentence, round numbers said the short way ("about a third"). Repeat the key word so a viewer who drifts off can get back in.
- Say the lesson twice where the lock-in style allows: promise it at 3–10 s, deliver it at the end.
- One line a viewer nods at (likes come from agreeing) and one worth sending to a friend (the lesson card).
- Pace comes from the writing: sentences of about 8–16 words that flow, joined into 5–9 paragraphs; every full stop and paragraph break adds a pause. Many short sentences and many numbers are what made episodes 5–8 slow (114–128 wpm).
- One feeling per episode (shock, fear, awe, amusement, "that could be me"), and the key moment built around it.
- Words and picture together: what is on screen is what is being said at that word (§6); a new picture at least every 2–3 s.
- Write it, then cut a fifth.

Format: `S = [(spoken text, [keywords]), …]`; each sentence lists 1–3 keywords, shown boxed in amber (a keyword written in capitals, "GOLD,", shows in capitals). `PARAS` groups sentences spoken in one breath; `PAUSE` is the silence after each paragraph (0.18–0.3 s). Force pronunciations with `[Mansa](/mˈɑnsə/)`. Also `PARTS`, `OPEN`, `CTA_ARM`, `HOOK`, `HOOK_SUB`, `BIG`, `BIG_WORD`, `LESSON`, `LESSON_USE`, `CTA`, `SHEET` and, rarely, `LONG`. `template/script.py` is episode 1 rewritten this way (it runs 70 s because it keeps every scene of the first cut; a new episode is 45–60 s).

From our own episodes:
- Episode 8 opened "In January 2026, silver crashed almost a third in one day." Better: "Silver lost almost a third of its value in one day." No date in front, and a huge percentage can stay a percentage: BIG `("−31%", "silver futures in one day", "30 Jan 2026")`.
- Episode 4's second sentence jumped to 1993 ("It began in 1993, in a diner in San Jose") before any question was open. Better: open the question first ("So why would investors sell a company that's winning?") and use the diner only as part of the answer.
- Episode 13 is the model for the middle: a short story, surprise 1 ("Same chips. Same sales forecast. Just one vote."), a rehook ("But here's the twist"), surprise 2 ("A fall needs a bigger rise to undo it.").

### 3.1 The first 10 seconds: the hook lab (Keith, 28 Nov 2026)
Keith: "用100种开头测试吧 包括前面两个1.5秒的hook 直到别人愿意看到10秒 … 你需要记住formula 然后一步步学". The opening is built from five parts; each part has levels; 5 × 2 × 4 × 3 × 5 = 600 different openings. Every Reel tests one level of each part at once, so each level is tried many times without 600 Reels.

| part | what it is | levels |
|---|---|---|
| `words` | the card text and the first sentence | contrast · number (the money figure first) · question · you (the viewer's own money) · scene (cold open in the moment) |
| `text` | what is written on the first frame | card (the big two-line hook card) · captions (no card, the first words as captions from frame 0) |
| `picture` | the camera at 0–1.5 s | place (wide shot of the main place) · action (the event already happening) · object (one big bright object) · person (one figure, close) |
| `beat2` | 1.5–3 s | dark (the big number over a dark screen) · world (the big number over the live world) · event (no number: a second thing happens) |
| `lock` | the lock-in, 3–10 s | stakes (how big, then the question) · lesson (what the viewer takes away) · flip (what most people believe, turned over) · name (the mechanism gets a name) · proof (one promised payoff with a number) |

- `python3 hooklab.py pick` (from the repo root, when claiming) prints the next episode's `OPEN` and `CTA_ARM` with the recipe and an example for every level (`hooklab/library.json`). Paste both lines into script.py and store the same values in the episode's `episodes.json` entry (`open`, `cta`). The same episode number always gets the same answer.
- Build what it picked. If one level truly cannot fit the story (a `you` hook on a 14th-century king), change only that part to another active level, say why in sources.md, and record what was really built: the record must match the video.
- For every opening: no date or name in front of the first sentence; something moves in the first half second; the hook card sits over sky or a dark part of the picture (a bright object goes below it). With a big number (`BIG`: money beats percentages, lost / wiped out / liquidated / paid, in billions or trillions, a percentage only when it is huge; crypto: the liquidation total, CoinGlass plus a news source; 2+ sources; its label says what and when) the first sentence says it while it is on screen (`BIG_WORD`, checked at 1.2–3 s).
- The score: the estimated share of viewers still watching at 10 s, worked out from Instagram's skip rate (left in the first 3 s), average watch time and the length; Instagram's API gives no retention curve, so it is an estimate. A Reel is scored at 12 h. `python3 hooklab.py` writes `trends/hooklab.md`: the formula so far (the best level of each part and how sure), every level's numbers, the CTA test, and the long road. The goal is 60% at 10 s over the last 10 Reels (28 Nov 2026: 38%).
- How it picks: every level twice first, then levels that score better more often, the others still get a turn now and then (Thompson sampling). With about 130 viewers a Reel, a 10-point difference between two levels shows after roughly 8 Reels each, a 5-point one after roughly 30: about two weeks for the big levers.
- Retiring and adding ("100 kinds"): when `trends/hooklab.md` says RETIRE, set that level's `status` to `"retired"` in `hooklab/library.json` and add one new level to the same part from its `ideas` list (new `id`, `since` = today, a clear `how` and `example`); if a new level needs template code, write it and copy it back to template/. One such change a run at most; say it in the report.
- First hints from episodes 9–25, labelled by hand afterwards (`hooklab/labels.json`; hints, no test): the two cards that started with the money figure (21, 23) lost 28% in 3 s against 43% for the others, and the first frames that were something other than a wide city shot (16, 21, 24) lost 28–35%.

## 4. CTA: a cheat sheet, two ways to hand it over (Keith, 28 Nov 2026)
Why: "Comment SELL or HOLD" asked for a set word and gave nothing back; in 25 Reels it brought no comment (the one comment we got was a real opinion, and nobody answered it). Instagram's @creators account said in 2024 that content asking people to comment a specific word, number or emoji is not recommended to non-followers, then added that genuinely engaging to give people something is not what it meant (Social Media Today, 2 and 5 Jun 2024). Instagram's head names watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top signals for Reels, and no comments. So: give something real, and make the lesson card the thing worth sending.
- Every episode has a cheat sheet, `SHEET` in script.py: the lesson made usable in 3–5 short lines (questions to ask, a small table, steps to check), 700 characters at most, plain text. No links, no other apps, never Bybit, nothing that tells anyone to buy, sell or hold, no promise, no prediction. It ends "(A story, not advice.)". `CTA = {"word", "get", "ask"}`: the keyword, what they get in a few words ("3 questions to ask first"), and the open question.
- Keith chose to alternate two arms for about two weeks (to about 12 Dec 2026) and then decide from `trends/hooklab.md`; `hooklab.py pick` gives the arm (even episode numbers K, odd O). From 12 Dec 2026 `trends/hooklab.md` says "Time to decide" under the CTA test and the report gives him the two rows (§10). When he picks, set `"cta": {"arm": "K"}` (or `"O"`) in `hooklab/library.json`: every episode then uses that arm.
  - **Arm K**: "Comment WORD, and I'll reply with <what they get>." WORD is 3–7 capital letters from the story. Picture: a comment box types the word, "Posted ✓", our reply pops up. Later runs reply to those comments with the SHEET (§9).
  - **Arm O**: "<What they get> is in the comments. <One open question about the lesson>?" No keyword. The question is about the viewer's own experience, answerable in a few words, with no right answer ("What's the biggest drop you ever sat through?"). Picture: a chip "in the comments · …" and the question as a card. Right after publishing, the SHEET goes up as our own comment (§8).
- History episodes use the same two arms; the old legend CTA is retired.
- No follow, DM or link talk until Keith says so (planned at 1,000 followers).
- Only the two things above are automatic. Keith has not approved automatic replies to ordinary comments: list those in the report so he can answer them himself.

## 5. Voice and sound
- Kokoro voice `bm_george` (British male, ~139 Hz, warm). `./tts.sh` picks the speed (0.95–1.2) that puts the whole video at about 160 wpm, caps pauses inside a paragraph at 0.3 s, and writes audio/vo_final.wav + timing.json (word times) and transcribes it with faster-whisper: every caption word must be heard. It prints `VIDEO <length> s, <words>, <wpm>` and `parts: …` (when each part starts), and stops with "FIX BEFORE RENDERING" (exit 2) when the length breaks §1 or a v3 piece is missing or wrong: `OPEN` and what it needs (HOOK for a card, BIG for a number), `PARTS`, a lock-in that ends after 12.5 s, `LESSON` and `LESSON_USE`, the CTA arm and its words, `SHEET`, advice wording, a link in the SHEET. Fix the WARN lines too (lock-in after 11 s, rehook outside 30–62%, a first sentence that does not match the picked `words`) unless there is a reason, noted in sources.md. Still under 150 wpm at 1.2: rewrite (§3, pace), don't force the voice. Then `./vo_proc.sh` (always re-run after any voice change).
- Kokoro needs Python ≤ 3.12: `setup.sh` puts it in ~/.venvs/tts; run the voice with `./tts.sh`. Whisper writes spoken numbers as digits ("£20,000"), so check hearing with `~/.venvs/tts/bin/python hearcheck.py <wav>`, which expands them. Where the American G2P misleads the British voice, force the vowel: `[stock](/stˈɒk/)` (heard as "stark" otherwise).
- `audio_build.py`: D-minor storybook bed (pads, plucked harp/oud arpeggios, kalimba, soft doum/tek drum in travel and market scenes), ambience (desert wind, market murmur, camel bells), a sound for every visual event (coins, pops, dings, whooshes on sky wipes); frame 0 gets a soft hit and the hook card a light whoosh when it lifts (`T.hookEnd`); the big number a slam, ticks while it counts and a ding when it lands (`T.bigIn`, `T.bigOut`); a beat-2 event its own hit (`T.beat2`); the lock-in promise a small bell; the rehook a low hit and a rising whoosh while the arpeggio stops for a breath; the lesson card a clear bell and a softer one for its check line; the CTA typing ticks, a "posted" blip and a reply blip (arm K) or a bell on the question (arm O). Bed about 14.5 dB under the voice with ducking. `./master.sh`: −14 LUFS, peak ≤ −2 dBFS.

## 6. Building the world (web/scene.js)
Start every episode with `./new_episode.sh NNN-slug` (copies `template/` = episode 1) and rewrite the story parts of `scene.js`; keep the engine parts.
- `kit.js`: `GB` merges primitives into one vertex-coloured mesh; ready models: person, camel, building, minaret, stall, palace, pyramid, tent, palm, boat, cloud, coins. Add new models to kit.js the same way (box/cyl/cone/ball, origin at ground centre, facing +x) and copy them back to template/.
- Story clock `T`: every event hangs off word times (`wt(sentence, 'word')`), never fixed seconds.
- Sky presets (night, dawn, morning, day, golden, sunset, dusk) keyed to the story; night adds stars, lit windows, lanterns, glow.
- Camera: spherical keys `{t, tg, d, az, el, up, mv}`; `tg` can follow a moving subject. Always slightly moving. Use a lift-over key when moving between places so the path never goes through buildings.
- The opening follows `OPEN` (timing.json carries it; `const OPEN` in scene.js; keep the template code: `TIM.hook`, `TIM.big`, `TIM.sub`, `fitHook()`, `T.hookEnd`, `T.bigIn`, `T.bigOut`, `T.beat2`). `text` card: the hook card at y 640 from frame 0, to 1.5 s when a number follows, else to the end of the hook sentence (3 s at most). `text` captions: no card; the first words show as captions from frame 0 and HOOK_SUB is a small chip at y 472. `beat2` dark: the big number at y 860 over a dark layer, 1.5–3 s. `beat2` world: the same number on a small plate at y 900 while the world stays bright. `beat2` event: no number; script a second event in the world at `T.beat2` (1.5 s). `picture` is the first camera keys: the template's `FIRST` shows all four on the same street (place, action, object, person), each ending on its own key at the first sky wipe so the camera never swings through a building. Frame 0 is the cover.
- Overlay: captions at top 300 px (≤3 words, said words dim, keyword amber), from the moment the card and the number have gone (words of sentence 1 said after that still get captions); one context chip at y 472 ("July 1324 · by the pyramids"); stamps for years, names, numbers; a rehook chip at y 412; the lesson card at y 960 from the word "lesson" to the CTA (`TIM.lesson`: label, two lines, the check line fading in under it); the CTA at y 890–1200 (`TIM.cta`: a chip "you get · …" or "in the comments · …"; arm K the comment box typing the keyword and our reply bubble, arm O the question card); a two-line source footnote at y 1556. Keep text out of Instagram's UI: bottom 330 px and the right 130 px from y 1000 down.
- Modern stories need modern sets (city blocks, trading floors, screens and phones as simple shapes, server rooms, charts as 3D lines). Build them in kit.js like the rest and copy them back to template/. Never draw a real logo, a real app or website screen, or a real person's likeness; names appear as text.
- Separate random streams (`rng(seed)`) for anything added late, so the rest of the world keeps its layout.
