# Sources (checked 20 Nov 2026, Malaysia time)
- Andrew Odlyzko, "Newton's financial misadventures in the South Sea Bubble", Notes and Records of the Royal Society 73 (2019) 25–59; revised version "Isaac Newton and the perils of the financial South Sea" (5 Mar 2020) read at https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/mania13c.pdf · and Odlyzko, Physics Today 73(7), 1 July 2020: https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/isaac-newton-and-the-perils-of-the-financial-south-sea
  - Newton was Warden of the Royal Mint from 1696 and Master of the Mint from 1699 until his death in 1727.
  - Start of 1720: slightly less than half his assets in South Sea stock (bought much earlier at 100 or below). He sold essentially his whole stake through instructions of 19 and 23 April 1720, "a profit of about £20,000 in the early stages of the Bubble".
  - He went back in with a series of investments in mid-June 1720, "close to their peak", kept buying (also in the August subscription); by the September collapse essentially his entire fortune was in South Sea.
  - Net worth "somewhat over £30,000" before the Bubble, "about £20,000" by mid-1721: "a loss of about £10,000"; "a famous anecdote claims he lost twice that much". Estate at his death in 1727 was again about £30,000.
  - Letter to Fatio de Duillier, 14 September 1724: "I lost very much by the South Sea company which makes my pockets empty" (Odlyzko calls the empty pockets an exaggeration).
- The National Archives (UK) blog, "The South Sea Bubble of 1720" (Dr Alice Marples, Newton Project, 2020): https://blog.nationalarchives.gov.uk/the-south-sea-bubble-of-1720 — almost £350 by the end of April 1720, nearly £600 by the end of May, £950 by the end of June, peak around £1,050, £190 in the late summer, £124 in December 1720; "Change Alley".
- University of Oxford, Newton and the Mint, "Newton and the South Sea Bubble": https://newtonandthemint.history.ox.ac.uk/global-financial-world/newton-and-the-south-sea-bubble — same price figures; April 1720 letter authorising his Mint deputy John Fauquier to sell Newton's stocks; says "it is likely that Newton lost some money, but the evidence is inconclusive" (debated point).
- Encyclopaedia Britannica, "South Sea Bubble": https://www.britannica.com/topic/South-Sea-Bubble — 128½ in January 1720, more than 1,000 in August, collapse by September, 124 by December.
- WIST quotations, "I can calculate the movement of the stars, but not the madness of the people" (Attributed): https://wist.info/newton-isaac/45097/ — earliest source is Joseph Spence's anecdotes (Lord Radnor's account): asked about the rising South Sea stock, Newton said he "could not calculate the madness of the people"; the "heavenly bodies" wording is a later embellishment.

Debated points the script attributes: Newton's trades ("Old records suggest"; Oxford calls the evidence inconclusive), the size of the loss (Odlyzko's estimate; the anecdote says twice as much), the madness line ("An old story says"). The chart joins the dated price figures with straight lines; the August point (£1,000) follows Britannica's "more than 1,000 in August".
