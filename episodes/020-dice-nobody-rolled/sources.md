# Episode 20 · The Dice Nobody Rolled (the Coldcard bitcoin wallet seed flaw, 2026)

Story: the Coldcard seed flaw. Coldcard hardware wallets (maker: Coinkite) are "cold" wallets, built so the secret keys stay offline. From firmware of March 2021 until 30 Jul 2026 a firmware setting silently switched off the chip's hardware random number generator for new wallet secrets (seeds); they came from a predictable software generator instead. Thieves rebuilt the keys on their own computers and, from 29-30 Jul 2026, swept bitcoin from wallets that never went online. Coinkite's 18-page postmortem (dated 23 Nov, published Tue 24 Nov 2026) counts 1,778.84 BTC from more than 8,600 addresses, says at least 15 reviews missed the flaw, and has no decision yet on compensation. Angle (new: the first episode on this story): the mechanism (how you rob a wallet that never goes online: guess its secret) and the latest twist (the postmortem: the checks confirmed the dice were in the box, never that anyone rolled them). No advice.

4. "Each secret should come from pure chance, like rolling dice." · tag "pure chance" · dice tumble in a glass box wired to the wallet
   - The seed should come from the STM32 chip's hardware random number generator (Bitcoin Magazine; FinanceFeeds); intended strength 128 bits (Bitcoin Magazine, Bitcoin.com, TRM). "Like rolling dice" is our picture of true randomness (seeds made with at least 50 private dice rolls were not weakened, Bitcoin Magazine).
5. "But for five years, a hidden setting swapped the dice for a recipe. And a recipe is far easier to guess." · card "MAR 2021 → JUL 2026 · 5 YEARS" · tags "a hidden setting", "dice: off", "a recipe", "easier to guess"
   - Bitcoin Magazine: firmware shipped from March 29, 2021 through July 30, 2026; seed generation "quietly fell back to Yasmarang, a predictable software generator built into MicroPython"; a firmware setting switched the hardware source off and a safeguard in the libNgU library failed to catch it; about 40 bits on Mk2/Mk3 and about 72 bits on Mk4/Mk5/Q instead of 128.
   - Bitcoin.com: firmware released March 29 (2021) "inadvertently directed wallet seed generation toward a software-based pseudorandom number generator rather than the intended hardware random-number generator"; "The defect was a silent substitution".
   - CoinDesk (Francisco Rodrigues, 17 Aug 2026, https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/08/17/how-a-bug-in-coldcard-s-code-went-unnoticed-for-years-leading-to-usd100-million-in-hacked-funds): the libngu library "checked only whether the setting existed, not whether it was on or off"; firmware 4.0.0 of 17 Mar 2021 / 4.0.1 the first affected release (so "Mar 2021" on screen, not a day).
   - TRM Labs: firmware 4.0.1 (March 2021) build configuration error; devices fell back to a weak software RNG; "as little as 40 bits".
   - "Swapped the dice for a recipe": our picture of the substitution. On the newer devices some hardware randomness still got in (CoinDesk: the software kept "only a small share"), so the newer models were weakened, not as badly as the older ones.
