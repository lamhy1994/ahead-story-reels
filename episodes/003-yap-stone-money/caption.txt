In 1903, a doctor heard of a family who were rich, though their money lay at the bottom of the sea.

On the island of Yap, big payments were made in giant stone wheels carved on Palau. One family's great stone sank in a storm on the way home, and the island agreed it still counted. Later, German officials fined the chiefs by painting black crosses on the finest stones.

Told from William Henry Furness's The Island of Stone Money (1910), as quoted by Milton Friedman. Historians still debate how well Western writers understood the stones.
