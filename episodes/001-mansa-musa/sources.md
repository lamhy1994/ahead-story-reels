# Sources (checked 19 Nov 2026)
- al-Umari's account of Mansa Musa in Cairo (gold gifts; mithqal fell from at least 25 to 22 dirhams or less; still low about twelve years later). English text: Boston University African Studies Center, "Kingdom of Mali – Primary Source Documents", citing Levtzion & Hopkins, Corpus of Early Arabic Sources for West African History (1981) pp. 269–273. https://www.bu.edu/africa/outreach/k_o_mali  · also https://www.digitalhistory.uh.edu/active_learning/explorations/1492/mansa_musa_visit.cfm
- Arrival in July 1324, three days by the pyramids, entered Cairo 19 July, about three months in Cairo; borrowed from Egyptian merchants at high interest on the way home (Ibn Battuta, Ibn al-Dawadari, al-Umari); al-Umari and Ibn Khaldun say the lenders were never or only partly repaid, other sources disagree; Warren Schultz argues the price drop was within normal fluctuation; "richest man" cannot be verified (Collet). Summarised from the Wikipedia article "Mansa Musa" (mirror read: cavac.at). 
- Strayer, Ways of the World ch. 9 (Macmillan): borrowed from Egyptian merchants at high interest rates on the return. https://digfir-published.macmillanusa.com/strayer3e/strayer3e_ch9_20.html
Debated points the script attributes: the size/length of the price drop ("One writer in Cairo said"), the unpaid loans ("some old accounts say"), "richest man in history" ("People still call him").
